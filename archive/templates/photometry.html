Extract photometric measurements around the specified sky point. The center field accepts sky coordinates or a resolvable object name, and the radius selects matching measurements around that position. Photometry searches default to 3 arcsec when no radius is provided, and submitted radii are limited to 5 arcmin.
Use the site, CCD, camera serial, filter, target ID, and night range fields to narrow the source images. Night values use YYYYMMDD. Leave quality cuts enabled for the cleaned light curve, or select Disable quality cuts to include all matching measurements.
The result view groups points by filter in an interactive light-curve plot with error bars and hover details. It also provides full and filtered text downloads, plus quick links to SIMBAD, AAVSO VSX, NSVS, ASAS-SN, and Aladin for the query position.
Search period computes a Lomb-Scargle periodogram of the very points displayed, down to the shortest period given beside it. Bands are pooled after centering each on its own median, as they sit whole magnitudes apart while the variability being looked for is far smaller. Clicking a peak folds the curve at that period; the dotted red lines mark the periods the observations themselves imprint on any periodogram — the day with its harmonics, the sidereal day and the year — and a peak sitting on one of those comes from the sampling rather than from the star. The solid green line is the period the curve is folded with, and the dashed green ones are the periods it would also appear at through the nightly sampling, at 1/(1/P ± n) per day: a real 0.8724 d signal necessarily produces peaks at 0.4659 and 6.8393 d as well, so a peak falling on a dashed line is that same period over again rather than a second one. The reported false alarm probability assumes pure noise and nothing else, so it is optimistic. Fold, Period and Epoch are applied in the browser, so editing them redraws immediately, and ÷2 / ×2 help with an eclipsing binary, whose periodogram peaks at half the true period.
